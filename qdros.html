The process

How it actually works

Four stages. Two of them depend on people other than me, which is why timelines vary more than anyone would like.

STAGE ONE

Review what you already have

Your judgment of divorce, your separation agreement or stipulation, and a recent statement for each account. I read the agreement first. If it is silent or ambiguous on survivor benefits, the valuation date, or how gains and losses are handled, that gets addressed before anything is drafted. Those problems are far harder to fix once an order is signed.

STAGE TWO

Drafting and plan review

I draft the order in the language your plan requires. Most administrators will review a draft before it goes to the court, and I revise until they accept it. Some plans do not offer that review, and the New York public retirement systems run their own process on their own schedule.

STAGE THREE

Signatures and entry

In most cases the order goes directly to the court for the judge’s signature and entry. Depending on the county and on what your agreement requires, your former spouse or their attorney may need to sign first, or the order may be submitted on notice to them. How long entry takes varies by county and is not something any attorney controls.

STAGE FOUR

Delivery and qualification

A certified copy goes to the plan administrator, who formally qualifies the order. Only then does the plan act on it, whether that means dividing an account or recording your interest in a pension that has not yet started paying.

Plan types

Plans I prepare orders for

New York public employees and union members often have two or three separate accounts that each require their own order. If that is your situation, say so when you call. Each additional order is priced lower than the first.

New York State & local retirement systems

NYSLRS (ERS and PFRS), NYSTRS, NYCERS, NYC Teachers, BERS, and the police and fire pension funds. These are governmental plans and use a Domestic Relations Order rather than a QDRO.

Deferred compensation

457(b) deferred compensation plans, which most New York public employees hold alongside their pension.

Union pension and annuity funds

Multiemployer pension funds, annuity funds and supplemental funds, frequently two or three separate orders for a single member.

Private employer plans

401(k), 403(b), ESOPs, profit sharing plans and defined benefit pensions governed by ERISA, including Fidelity, Vanguard, Empower, TIAA and Principal.

IRAs

An IRA does not require a QDRO. It is divided under the transfer incident to divorce rules instead. In practice most custodians still want a court certified order before they will split an account, and each one has its own forms and review process. I prepare the order to divide the IRA and the transmittal package that goes with it, often alongside the QDROs in the same matter.

Common questions

What people ask before they call

How much does a QDRO cost in New York?

It depends on how many retirement accounts are being divided and what kind of plans they are, so I quote it on the first call rather than guess here. It is a flat fee per order, given to you in writing before you retain, and it covers drafting, plan review and coordinating entry with the court.

Online document services charge a few hundred dollars, and it is worth knowing what that buys. They produce a document. They do not review your agreement, submit the order to the plan, revise it when the plan objects, or get it signed by a judge.

Who pays for the QDRO?

It depends on your settlement. Some agreements split the cost, some assign it to one spouse, and some are silent, in which case the person who needs the order done usually pays for it. Whoever retains this office is responsible for the fee, regardless of what a court later orders the other side to contribute.

How long does it take?

Drafting takes a few days once I have your documents. The longer part is outside anyone's control: plan administrators typically take four to eight weeks to review an order, and court entry adds a few more. Public retirement systems are often slower. Getting your paperwork to me promptly is the single biggest thing that shortens the timeline.

My spouse has a New York State pension. Is that different?

Yes. New York's public retirement systems are governmental plans, outside of ERISA, so the order is technically a Domestic Relations Order and each system has its own required language and review process. I prepare these regularly. Many public employees also hold a 457(b) deferred compensation account, which needs its own separate order.

My divorce was finalized years ago. Is it too late?

Usually not, and it is worth acting quickly. Until the order is entered and accepted by the plan, your share is exposed. The participant can retire, take a distribution, remarry, or die, and depending on the plan and what your agreement says, you can lose part or all of what you were awarded.

Before you call

The matters I take

I prepare orders in matters that are already settled. That means your judgment of divorce or your stipulation already sets out how the retirement accounts are to be divided, and your former spouse is not fighting the terms of the order.

Most QDRO work fits that description. The order simply carries out a division the parties already agreed to, and the other side has no reason to object. Those matters move predictably, and the flat fee covers them from start to finish.

If your former spouse is contesting the division itself, if the account has already been emptied, or if the matter will require motion practice, that is litigation rather than order preparation. I do not take those matters on a flat fee, and in most cases you are better served by a matrimonial litigator. Tell me on the first call and I will point you somewhere useful.
